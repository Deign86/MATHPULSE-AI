###### Lesson 1.1. Computing Salary, Wage, and Commission

**Objectives:** At the end of the lesson, you should be able to:

1. differentiate among annual salary, hourly wage, commission, and piecework income;
2. accurately compute weekly and monthly wages from an annual salary and hourly rate including overtime pay and allowance;
3. calculate earnings from commission and piecework; and
4. solve word problem involving weekly and monthly wages from an annual salary and hourly rate including overtime pay and allowance

**Key Ideas and Wage means the remuneration or earnings, however designated, capable of being**

**Examples:** expressed in terms of money, whether fixed or ascertained on a time, task, piece,

or commission basis, which is payable by an employer to an employee under a written or unwritten contract of employment for work done or to be done, or for services rendered or to be rendered.

- Hourly wage is the amount earned for each hour worked.
- Daily wages are the amount of money a worker earns for each day of actual work. It does not include payment for days the employee did not work (e.g., rest days, holidays, or leaves), unless otherwise required by law or employer policy.

**Salary refers to a fixed, regular payment-typically distributed monthly, semi-monthly,**

or weekly-offered to employees regardless of the exact number of hours worked. It is standard for professional, managerial, supervisory, or administrative positions.

- Annual salary is the total gross amount of money an employee earns in a full calendar year before tax deductions.
- Monthly Salary is the fixed amount an employee earns each calendar month.

(DOLE use the Factor 313 days for a 6-day workweek, and 261 days for a 5-day workweek).

- A semi-monthly salary is the amount of money paid to a worker twice a

month. By law, the payment intervals must not exceed sixteen (16) days.

- Weekly salary is the income received or computed for a single workweek.

###### Gross income is the total earnings before any deductions are made. Includes base

salary, overtime, commission, and piecework pay.

###### Gross income = Salary + Allowances

**Net Pay is also called "take-home pay." This is the amount left after deductions are**

subtracted from gross pay.

###### Net Pay = Gross Income - Total Deductions

###### Deductions are the amounts subtracted from gross pay by law or agreement. In the

Philippines, common deductions include:

###### 1. Social Security System (SSS)

*Applicable to: Private sector employees Purpose: Retirement, disability, maternity, sickness, and death benefits Rate: Based on a schedule tiered to the employee's monthly gross income.*

The total SSS contribution rate is 15% of the employee's designated Monthly Salary Credit (MSC). This total percentage is split between the worker and the company:

- Employee Share: 5%
- Employer Share: 10%

SSS has strict boundaries that dictate how wages are categorized. *The Minimum Cap (Income Floor): ₱5,000.00 MSC* - If an employee's gross monthly salary is less than ₱5,250.00, their wage

defaults to the lowest bracket base of ₱5,000.00. - Absolute Minimum Employee Share: ₱5,000.00 × 5% = ₱250.00 *The Maximum Cap (Income Ceiling): ₱35,000.00 MSC* - If an employee earns an exceptionally high salary (e.g., ₱50,000.00 or

₱100,000.00), their premium computation hits the maximum bracket base of ₱35,000.00. - Absolute Maximum Employee Share: ₱35,000.00 × 5% = ₱1,750.00

Step-by-Step Payroll Computation

To determine an employee's exact deduction during payroll processing, follow this workflow:

1. *Calculate the Gross Monthly Earnings: Sum the base salary and any taxable* allowances or overtime pay received during the month.
2. Find the Income Bracket: Locate the official SSS Contribution Table and find where the gross income fits along the "Range of Compensation" column. [https://www.sss.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2025-SSS-](https://www.sss.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2025-SSS-Contribution-Table-rev.pdf) [Contribution-Table-rev.pdf](https://www.sss.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2025-SSS-Contribution-Table-rev.pdf)
3. Identify the MSC: Look at the corresponding Monthly Salary Credit (MSC) assigned to that specific bracket.
4. Multiply by the Employee Rate: Multiply that specific MSC by 5% to find the exact salary deduction.

###### 2. Government Service Insurance System (GSIS)

*Applicable to: Government employees only* *Purpose: Life insurance, retirement, disability, and survivorship benefits* *Rate: 9% of monthly salary rounded to the nearest peso (employee share)* Under Republic Act No. 8291 (the GSIS Act), mandatory monthly contributions are computed as a direct percentage of the employee's Actual Monthly Basic Salary (MBS). The total mandatory contribution rate is 21%, split as follows:

- Employee (Personal) Share: 9% of the actual Monthly Basic Salary.
- Employer (Government) Share: 12% of the actual Monthly Basic Salary. There is no maximum salary ceiling or income cap for regular GSIS members. Additionally, there is no fixed statutory "floor amount."

-----

GSIS membership is compulsory for all government personnel who receive compensation and hold a recognized appointment, regardless of their employment status. This includes regular employees and casual employees. Job Orders (JO), Contract of Service (COS) workers, and uniformed personnel are not covered by GSIS.

###### 3. Pag-IBIG Fund (HDMF)

*Applicable to: All employees* *Purpose: Housing loans, savings, and calamity loans* *Rate: 2% of monthly salary rounded to the nearest peso (employee share).* Pag-IBIG (HDMF) has a strict minimum baseline and a maximum cap. The parameters are governed by Pag-IBIG Circular No. 460, which outlines the mandatory contribution rules based on an employee's Monthly Fund Salary (MFS) or Regular Income. *The Maximum Fund Salary cap is exactly ₱10,000.00.*

- Any income earned above ₱10,000.00 is completely ignored for the mandatory calculation.
- Because the standard rate for incomes over ₱1,500 is 2%, the maximum deduction is locked: - Maximum Employee Share: 10,000.00 × 2% = ₱200.00 - Maximum Employer Share: 10,000.00 × 2% = ₱200.00 - *Total Maximum Remittance: ₱400.00* *The Minimum Cap*
- There isn't a "floor" that forces low-income earners to pay a higher flat amount (unlike PhilHealth's ₱10,000 floor). Instead, the minimum rules change the percentage rate applied to actual earnings: - Employees earning ₱1,500.00 or less per month qualify for a reduced Pag- IBIG deduction rate of 1% of their monthly basic contract pay. The employer is still legally required to pay a matching share of 2%. Despite the set cap, Pag-IBIG allows employees to voluntarily contribute extra money beyond the standard limit to build up personal savings and retirement investments.

###### 4. PhilHealth

*Applicable to: All employees* *Purpose: Healthcare benefits and hospitalization coverage* *Rate: 5% of monthly salary, shared between employee and employer* The total premium rate for PhilHealth Contribution is 5% of the Monthly Basic Salary (MBS) or the Regular Income, which is split equally (2.5% each) between the employee and the employer. However, the calculation is strictly bound by these limits:

*The Minimum Cap (Income Floor): ₱10,000.00*

- If an employee earns less than ₱10,000.00 a month, their contribution is calculated as if they earned exactly ₱10,000.00. - Minimum Total Premium: ₱500.00 per month - Minimum Employee Share: ₱250.00 *The Maximum Cap (Income Ceiling): ₱100,000.00*
- If an employee earns more than ₱100,000.00 a month, their contribution is capped exactly at the ₱100,000 limit. - Maximum Total Premium: ₱5,000.00 per month - Maximum Employee Share: ₱2,500.00

-----

Republic Act No. 11223 (Universal Health Care Act) is the current legal mandate that explicitly governs the progressive increase of PhilHealth premium collections up to the 5% rate along with the designated salary floor and ceiling limits.

###### 5. Withholding Tax (BIR)

*Applicable to: All employees* *Purpose: To deduct and remit an employee's income tax liabilities progressively* *Rate: Based on monthly salary bracket and applicable exemptions for tax rate.* [Refer to this link https://mpm.ph/bir-tax-table/#incometaxtable .](https://mpm.ph/bir-tax-table/#incometaxtable) The tax charged directly to a person's income, salary, or wages is called the Withholding Tax on Compensation (often referred to broadly as Individual Income Tax). It is called a withholding tax because the employee doesn't have to manually pay it to the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) every month. Instead, the employer is legally mandated to deduct (withhold) the tax directly from the employee's paycheck and remit it to the government on their behalf. The Philippines uses a progressive tax system regulated by the TRAIN Law (RA 10963- Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion). Under this system, those who earn more are taxed at higher percentage rates, while lower earners are exempted entirely. The tax is calculated using a structured tiered table.

Step-by-Step Payroll Computation

1. Determine Gross Compensation Income: Base Salary + Overtime + Allowances.
2. Deduct Non-Taxable Items: Subtract the employee's statutory contributions *(SSS or GSIS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG shares). These are strictly non-* *taxable.*

4. Apply the Bracket Table: Look up the Taxable Income amount in the monthly tax bracket table to calculate the exact deduction.

###### SCIENTIFIC NOTATION

Scientific notation is a mathematical method used to express very large or very small numbers in a shorter and more manageable form.

It is written as:

× where:

- is a number from 1 to less than 10
- is an integer exponent indicating the power of 10.

###### Converting Standard Form to Scientific Notation

-----

###### To convert a number to scientific notation:

1. Move the decimal point, so the number becomes from 1 to less than 10.
2. Count how many places the decimal point moved.
3. If the decimal moves to the left, the exponent is positive.
4. If the decimal moves to the right, the exponent is negative. Examples:
- A virus has a diameter of 0.00000006 meter. Express the measurement in scientific notation.

This is a very small measurement, so scientific notation makes it easier to write and understand. A more appropriate unit is nanometer (nm) because it is used for extremely small measurements. This can also be expressed as

**60 nm.**

- A fully loaded truck has a mass of 12,000,000 grams. Express the mass in scientific notation.

The mass of the loaded truck is 1.2 × 10⁷ grams, which is close to the mega (10⁶) scale, so it can be expressed in megagrams. In daily life, this is commonly written as a metric ton (t) when referring to large vehicle loads or heavy objects. Thus, the measurement can be expressed more conveniently as 12 megagrams (Mg) or 12

**metric tons (t). Converting Scientific Notation to Standard Form**

###### To convert scientific notation to standard form:

1. Identify the exponent.
2. Move the decimal point to the right if the exponent is positive.
3. Move the decimal point to the left if the exponent is negative.
4. Write the resulting number.

###### READING AND INTERPRETING SCALES

A scale shows the relationship between a measurement on a drawing (map, blueprint, or sketch) and the actual measurement in real life. It helps represent large distances or objects in a smaller, manageable form while keeping correct proportions. Scales are commonly represented in three ways:

1. Unit Scale. A unit scale compares two different units of measurement.

###### Example: 1 cm = 100 m

This means that every 1 centimeter on the map represents 100 meters in real life.

2. Ratio Scale. A ratio scale compares measurements using the same unit.

**Example: 1: 10,000**

This means that 1 unit in the drawing represents 10,000 of the same units in real

life.

Rubric evaluates Community Context and Socio-Cultural Responsiveness, Physics Application and Scientific Justification, Scale, Measurement, Conversion, and Representation, Communication and Advocacy, Collaboration and Healthy Relationships, and Integration of Learning Areas and Advocacy Brochure. The Engagement, Reflection, and Personal Growth Assessment evaluates learners' role performance, project engagement, self-reflection, and personal growth. Invited stakeholders may also provide feedback on the relevance, responsiveness, feasibility, potential impact, and persuasiveness of the proposed innovation.

Learners will have successfully completed the task when they present a communitybased innovation that is relevant, feasible, evidence-based, and responsive to local needs; demonstrate accurate application of scientific, mathematical, communication, and socio-cultural concepts; collaborate effectively with others; and communicate a clear and persuasive advocacy message supported by appropriate evidence and reasoning.
