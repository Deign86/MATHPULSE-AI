###### B.2. Discussing the Concept

###### Activity B.2: Lesson Discussion of Sequences

Instructions: Study the information and examples and prepare for class discussion.

###### Key Terms and Definitions Sequence - a list of numbers arranged in a specific

order, following a rule. *Example: 3, 6, 12, 24, . . .*

**Term - each number in a sequence.**

*Example: In the sequence 3, 6, 12, 24, each of the* numbers 3, 6, 12, and 24 is a term.

###### Position (or Index) - the place of a term in the

sequence, usually counted as 1st term, 2nd term, and so on

###### Notation: 1 is the first term, 2 is the second term,

etc. In general, denotes the nth term

###### Rule (or Pattern Rule) - a description of how the terms

is formed or how they change from one to the next. *Example: "Add 2" in the sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, …*

###### Fibonacci Sequence - a sequence where each term

after the first two is the sum of the two preceding terms. *Example: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …*

###### Classification of Sequences

(based on the number of terms)

###### Finite Sequence

A finite sequence has a specific number of terms - it has a beginning and an end.

###### Activity B.2: Lesson Discussion

In this part, students will study key terms and examples related to sequences in preparation for a guided class discussion. They will learn essential vocabulary such as term, position, rule, and notation, as well as recognize different types of sequences including finite, infinite, and the Fibonacci sequence. This activity builds the foundational understanding needed to analyze, extend, and represent sequences mathematically, forming a crucial step toward deeper pattern recognition and generalization in succeeding lessons.

To facilitate the discussion, the teacher may select any of the given strategies or any strategy that suits the needs of the learners. Additional examples may be provided if necessary

An infinite sequence has no end- it goes on forever. It keeps following the same rule endlessly. You can describe the rule, but you can't list all the terms.

*Example:* 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, … (This keeps doubling and never stops.)

In mathematical notation, an infinite sequence often includes ellipsis "…" to show it continues.

###### Strategy 3: Peer Teaching

Instructions:

1. Create teaching stations for each term with its definition.

5. After the given time, rotate the groups to the next station.
6. When all groups have visited each station, bring the class together to discuss any challenges or findings.
